Video has become one of the main ways buyers get to know companies. Before a first meeting, many prospects will watch a product explainer, a short introduction from the team or a clip from an event. A company with no video can feel harder to understand - and harder to trust.

But more video is not always better. Many businesses invest in long corporate films that few people watch to the end. The most effective video is usually shorter, clearer and planned for the places it will actually be seen.

## Why video matters for business buyers

Video does things text struggles to do:

- **It explains complex products quickly**, through demonstration rather than description.
- **It shows the people behind the company**, which builds familiarity and trust.
- **It holds attention** in busy feeds and inboxes.
- **It travels well** - a good clip is easy to share with colleagues.

## Why long videos often fail

Long corporate films usually try to say everything: history, values, products, people and ambitions. The result is a video that is impressive to make and difficult to watch. Viewers drop off early, and the most important message is often buried in the middle.

> The best business videos answer one question well, rather than many questions briefly.

## Plan by purpose, not by length

Start with the job each video needs to do:

- **Explain** - how a product works or what a service involves.
- **Introduce** - who the team is and how it works.
- **Prove** - how a project was delivered and what changed.
- **Invite** - encourage people to visit a stand, attend an event or book a call.

Each purpose suggests a different length and style. An explainer may need two minutes; an invitation may need fifteen seconds.

## One shoot, many assets

The most efficient way to produce video is to plan a single shoot that creates many pieces of content. A well-planned day can produce:

- A short brand film for the website.
- Several short clips for social media.
- Product explainers for sales teams.
- Vertical versions for mobile platforms.
- Stills for presentations and articles.

This approach keeps costs down and keeps the visual style consistent across channels.

## Make it work without sound

Many people watch video with the sound off, especially on social platforms and in offices. Clear captions, strong visuals and on-screen text make sure the message lands either way.

## Keep it on-brand

Video should look and feel like the rest of your brand: the same colours, typography, tone and level of quality. Inconsistent video can undermine an otherwise strong identity.

## Measure what matters

Views are only a starting point. More useful signals include how much of each video people watch, which videos appear in sales conversations and whether video pages lead to enquiries.

## The bottom line

Video is now part of how buyers judge companies. Keep it focused, plan for the channels where it will be seen, produce many assets from each shoot and stay on-brand. Short, clear and purposeful almost always beats long and comprehensive.
