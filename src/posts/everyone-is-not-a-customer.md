Ask a leadership team who their customer is and you will often hear some version of "anyone who needs what we do." It sounds ambitious. In practice it is one of the most expensive positions a company can take, because a message designed for everyone is relevant to no one in particular.

Focus is uncomfortable. It feels like turning away revenue. Yet the companies that grow fastest are almost always the ones that are clearest about who they are for - and, just as importantly, who they are not for.

## Why "everyone" costs so much

When the audience is vague, every marketing decision gets harder and more expensive.

- **Messages become generic.** To avoid excluding anyone, language drifts towards safe, empty phrases that could belong to any competitor.
- **Channels multiply.** If everyone is a target, every channel looks relevant, and the budget is spread too thin to make an impact anywhere.
- **Sales cycles stretch.** Prospects who are a poor fit take longer to decide, ask for more customisation and churn faster.
- **Price pressure rises.** Without a clear reason to choose you, buyers compare on price.

The result is a company that works very hard to stay average.

## Focus is not exclusion; it is a sharper hook

Choosing a focused audience does not mean refusing business from others. It means designing your message, website and sales process for the buyers you serve best. Others can still find you and buy from you; they simply are not the people you shape everything around.

A sharp focus creates a strong hook. When a prospect reads your homepage and thinks "this was written for companies exactly like mine," trust forms instantly. That feeling is impossible to create when you are trying to speak to everyone at once.

> The narrower the description, the stronger the recognition. People buy from the company that seems to understand them best.

## How to choose your focus

The best focus usually sits where three things overlap:

1. **Where you already win.** Look at your best customers - the ones who pay well, stay long and recommend you. What do they have in common?
2. **Where you have real expertise.** Which problems can your team solve better than almost anyone else?
3. **Where there is enough demand.** Is the group large enough to sustain growth for the next few years?

Write the answer as a short profile: the type of company, its size or stage, the role of the buyer and the problem that brings them to you. Then test it against your sales history. If your best deals fit the profile, you have found your focus.

## What changes once you choose

A clear focus improves almost everything downstream:

- **Your website** can speak in the buyer's language and answer their exact questions.
- **Your content** can go deep on problems your audience actually faces.
- **Your sales material** can include examples and details that feel immediately relevant.
- **Your outreach** becomes more personal and more effective, because you know whom to contact and why.
- **Your pricing** becomes easier to defend, because you are no longer a generic option.

Marketing spend goes further when it is concentrated. A smaller, well-chosen audience reached repeatedly will usually outperform a large, vague one reached once.

## The fear of missing out

The most common objection is the fear of losing business outside the focus. In reality, very little is lost. Companies outside your core profile still find you through referrals and search, and many of them will still buy. What changes is that your marketing stops being diluted by trying to please them.

If the focus later proves too narrow, you can widen it deliberately - which is far easier than trying to narrow a vague brand after years of generic messaging.

## A simple exercise for this week

Gather your leadership team and complete these three sentences:

- "We are the best choice for..."
- "We are not the right choice for..."
- "Our best customers come to us when..."

If the answers are vague or inconsistent, that is the most valuable marketing problem you can solve this quarter. Clear answers make every brand, website and campaign decision faster and cheaper.

## The bottom line

"Everyone" feels safe because it avoids difficult choices. But in marketing, avoiding choices is the most expensive choice of all. Decide who you serve best, design everything around them and let that clarity do the selling.
