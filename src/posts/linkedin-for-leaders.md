Many leaders feel pressure to be active on LinkedIn. They see peers posting daily, sharing achievements and commenting on every trend. Some try to keep up and quickly run out of time and ideas. Others decide it is not for them and disappear entirely.

There is a better middle path. Leaders do not need to post often to build credibility. They need to post with substance.

## Why leader presence matters

Buyers, partners, investors and future employees increasingly look up company leaders before engaging with a business. A thoughtful, consistent presence signals a company with clear thinking and confident leadership. In many industries, people trust individuals more than corporate accounts.

But presence only helps if it is credible. Generic motivational posts and constant self-promotion can do more harm than good.

> Influence comes from being useful to the people you want to reach - not from being visible to everyone.

## Decide who you are speaking to

Before writing anything, define the audience. For most executives, it is a small group: potential clients, partners, senior hires and industry peers. Writing for them specifically makes every post sharper and more relevant.

## Choose three themes

Pick three topics you can speak about with genuine authority - for example, the future of your industry, lessons from building the company and how your clients can avoid common mistakes. Staying within these themes builds a recognisable point of view.

## Post less, but better

One thoughtful post every week or two is enough for most leaders. Each post should do at least one of these:

- **Share a clear opinion** on something your audience cares about.
- **Explain a lesson** learned from real experience.
- **Answer a question** clients frequently ask.
- **Make a complex topic simple.**

Avoid posting just to stay visible. Silence is better than noise.

## Write like a person

The most effective leadership posts sound human. Use plain language, short paragraphs and a clear first line that earns attention. Share specifics rather than slogans. Admit uncertainty where it exists - it builds trust.

## Engage with intention

Commenting thoughtfully on posts by clients, partners and peers is often more valuable than publishing. A considered comment can start a conversation that a broadcast post never would. Spend a few minutes a week engaging with the people who matter most to your business.

## Let your team help - carefully

Busy leaders can work with their marketing team or an agency to capture ideas and draft posts. The key is authenticity: the ideas, opinions and stories must be the leader's own. A short weekly conversation can provide material for several posts, edited to sound like the leader, not a brand account.

## Measure the right things

Likes and follower counts are poor indicators of value. Better signs include:

- Messages from potential clients or partners.
- Prospects mentioning your posts in meetings.
- Invitations to speak or contribute.
- Candidates citing your posts as a reason they applied.

## The bottom line

Leaders do not need to become content creators. They need to share clear, useful thinking with the right people, consistently and authentically. Post less, say more - and let credibility compound over time.
