Many company websites are written in a language no customer actually speaks. Internal terms, polished slogans and industry jargon fill the pages, while the phrases buyers use to describe their real problems are nowhere to be found.

The fix is surprisingly simple: listen to your best customers and let their words shape your message.

## Why customer language works

Buyers search, think and talk in their own words. When they read a website that uses the same language, they feel understood immediately. When they read a website full of internal vocabulary, they have to translate - and many will not bother.

Customer language also tends to be more concrete and emotional than corporate copy. A customer might say "we were tired of chasing three different suppliers," while a website says "integrated multi-disciplinary solutions." Only one of those phrases makes a buyer nod.

> Your customers have already written your best headline. You just have not heard it yet.

## Where to find the words

You do not need a large research project. Rich customer language is usually available in places you already have:

- **Sales calls.** Listen to how prospects describe their problem in the first ten minutes.
- **Interviews with happy clients.** Ask why they chose you and what changed afterwards.
- **Emails and enquiries.** Notice the phrases people use when they first get in touch.
- **Reviews and feedback.** Look for repeated words and themes.
- **Lost deals.** Ask what made the decision difficult - their answers reveal hidden doubts.

## Questions that unlock great language

When speaking with customers, a few questions consistently produce useful phrases:

1. What was happening in your business before you started looking for help?
2. What had you already tried, and why did it not work?
3. What nearly stopped you from choosing us?
4. What changed after we started working together?
5. How would you describe us to a colleague?

Record the answers word for word. The exact phrasing matters.

## Turn phrases into pages

Once you have a collection of customer language, look for patterns:

- **Recurring problems** become headlines and section openings.
- **Common doubts** become FAQ answers and reassurance on key pages.
- **Described outcomes** become the promises in your positioning.
- **Comparisons** to alternatives become your differentiation.

Use customers' words directly where possible, refined only for clarity. With permission, a client's own description of a problem can be more persuasive than any copywriter's version.

## Keep listening

Markets change, and so does the language buyers use. Make customer listening a regular habit rather than a one-off exercise. Ask your sales team to note memorable phrases from conversations, and review them every quarter for new insights.

## The bottom line

The clearest, most persuasive version of your message already exists - in the mouths of the customers who chose you. Capture their words, use them to shape your website and materials, and buyers will recognise themselves in every page.
