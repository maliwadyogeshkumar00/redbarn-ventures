Competitive research does not need expensive reports. Some of the most useful insight about your market is sitting in plain sight on your competitors' websites. Every homepage, service page and case description reveals what they believe matters to buyers - and, just as importantly, what they leave out.

An hour spent reading your market this way can sharpen your positioning more than a month of internal workshops.

## Set up the exercise

Choose five competitors: three you lose deals to most often and two you consider aspirational. Open their websites side by side with your own. For each one, capture the same information in a simple table:

- The main homepage headline.
- Who they say they serve.
- The problems they claim to solve.
- How they prove credibility.
- The primary call to action.
- The tone of voice.

The table makes patterns visible very quickly.

## Look for the sameness

In most markets, competitors sound remarkably alike. The same promises appear again and again: quality, innovation, partnership, customer focus. When everyone makes the same claim, the claim stops meaning anything.

Circle every phrase that appears on three or more websites, including your own. These are the words buyers have learned to ignore. Your positioning should avoid them - or make them specific enough to be believable.

> If your homepage headline could be pasted onto a competitor's site without anyone noticing, it is not working hard enough.

## Find the unanswered questions

Next, think like a buyer. What questions would you need answered before contacting any of these companies? Common examples include:

- How long does a typical project take?
- What does working together actually look like?
- What happens after launch or delivery?
- Who will I really be working with?
- What does it roughly cost, or what drives the cost?

If competitors avoid these questions, answering them clearly on your website is an immediate advantage. Buyers reward the company that removes uncertainty.

## Spot the positioning gaps

Look for spaces nobody occupies:

- **An audience nobody speaks to directly.** Perhaps everyone targets large enterprises, leaving ambitious mid-sized firms underserved.
- **A problem nobody names.** Competitors describe services, but nobody describes the frustration buyers actually feel.
- **A promise nobody makes.** For example, a clear process, a single point of contact or ongoing support.
- **A tone nobody uses.** If the whole market sounds formal and distant, warmth and plain language stand out.

Each gap is a potential position. The best ones match something you already do well.

## Compare the experience, not just the words

Visit each site on your phone. Check how fast it loads, how easy it is to find contact details and how the site handles a real question. Submit an enquiry if appropriate and note how quickly and how well each company responds. The experience often reveals more about a competitor's operations than anything they write.

## Turn insight into action

Finish the exercise with three decisions:

1. **One phrase to remove** from your own website because it sounds like everyone else.
2. **One buyer question to answer** that competitors ignore.
3. **One position to test** - a focus, promise or tone that sets you apart.

Implement them, then repeat the exercise every six months. Markets move, and so should your understanding of them.

## The bottom line

Your competitors are publishing their strategy every day. Read it carefully, notice where everyone sounds the same and step into the space nobody owns. Differentiation rarely requires inventing something new - only saying something true that others are not saying.
