Most companies publish content because they feel they should. A blog post here, a social update there, often written quickly and focused on the company itself. The result is content that nobody reads and nobody remembers.

The content that actually wins business works differently. It helps the buyer understand a problem, make a decision or avoid a mistake. In doing so, it demonstrates expertise so clearly that the reader starts to trust the author before any sales conversation begins.

## The expertise test

Before publishing anything, ask one question: "Would a potential client find this useful even if they never bought from us?"

If the answer is yes, the content is likely to build trust. If the answer is no - because it is mainly about your company, your awards or your products - it is advertising, and readers will treat it that way.

> Helpful content earns attention. Promotional content has to buy it.

## What useful business content looks like

The most effective content usually falls into a few categories:

- **Explanations.** How something works, in plain language, for people who need to understand it.
- **Decision guides.** How to choose between options, including when you are not the right choice.
- **Mistake warnings.** The common errors buyers make and how to avoid them.
- **Process transparency.** What really happens during a project, step by step.
- **Honest answers to sensitive questions.** What drives cost, how long things take, what can go wrong.

Each of these answers a question buyers are already asking - often in search engines and, increasingly, in AI assistants.

## Write for the person signing the cheque

Business content often targets specialists and forgets the decision-maker. Senior buyers want clarity, brevity and relevance to business outcomes. Write so a busy executive can understand the main point in the first two paragraphs, with detail available for those who want it.

## Show your thinking, not just your conclusions

Readers trust experts who explain their reasoning. Rather than stating "this is best practice," explain why it matters, when it applies and when it does not. Nuance signals experience; absolutes often signal marketing.

## Consistency beats volume

Publishing one genuinely useful piece each month for a year will usually achieve more than a burst of rushed posts followed by silence. Consistency builds an archive of helpful material that compounds in search, in sales conversations and in reputation.

Practical tips:

- Keep a list of questions customers ask in sales calls - each is a potential article.
- Involve your experts through short interviews, then let a writer shape the result.
- Repurpose each piece into posts, slides and email content.
- Link articles to relevant service pages so readers can take the next step.

## Measure trust, not just traffic

Page views are useful, but the real value of content shows up elsewhere:

- Prospects mentioning articles in first conversations.
- Sales teams sending content to answer common questions.
- Enquiries that arrive already understanding your approach.
- Time spent reading and returning visitors.

When content starts appearing in the sales process, it is working.

## The bottom line

Content sells best when it does not try to sell. Answer the questions your buyers are asking, explain your thinking openly and publish consistently. Over time, your expertise becomes visible - and trust follows.
